# RoutineNest privacy policy draft

Publisher name, contact email, public policy URL and effective date require publisher approval before publication.

RoutineNest stores routine names, task names, weekday schedules, reminder times and completion dates locally on the device. Appearance, language and speech preferences are also stored locally. No account is required for core routines, and no advertising or app analytics trackers are intentionally included. An export requested by the user writes routine data to the destination they choose; the app's deletion control removes routines and completion history and cancels its reminders. Premium purchase records, if enabled, are handled separately and are not erased by deleting routines.

Microphone permission is optional and requested when voice input is used. The device's chosen speech recognition service may transmit audio/transcripts to its provider, under that provider's privacy terms. Text commands provide equivalent supported routine actions without microphone access. Optional text-to-speech uses the installed engine, which may have its own data practices. Speech input is not continuously recorded by RoutineNest.

Notifications are optional. Reminders use local device scheduling, subject to permission, battery and operating-system delivery limits. No exact-delivery guarantee is made.

When premium purchasing is configured, Amazon handles checkout and account/purchase information under Amazon's terms. A publisher-operated verification server will validate receipt identifiers against Amazon RVS. Its data retention, lawful processing, access control and contact details must be specified before enabling it. The development build has no configured verification server and does not unlock unverified purchases.

Android app backups are disabled in the manifest. Device-transfer behaviour varies by device/vendor and must be verified during release testing. Do not promise that vendor tools can never copy local data.

The publisher must review all SDK data processing, retention and the Amazon privacy questionnaire against the final binary. This draft is engineering documentation and is not a published or legally approved privacy policy.
